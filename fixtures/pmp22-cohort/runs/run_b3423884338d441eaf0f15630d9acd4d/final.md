Conclusion: miR-29-dependent PMP22 repression is supported in Schwann-cell cultures, and IRE1-linked PMP22 destabilization is supported in mouse fibroblasts. Neither establishes that a particular Schwann-cell RNA decrease is caused by increased decay rather than reduced synthesis or altered cell state.

Evidence that changes the starting position

1. miR-29/AGO: In newborn-rat Schwann cultures, miR-29a represses PMP22, anti-miR relieves repression, and deleting the reporter’s predicted seed site removes its response. Transfected Ago2 associates with PMP22 RNA. These establish sequence-dependent regulation, but not endogenous AGO-loading efficiency, AGO2 necessity, or the fraction attributable to decay versus translation. Source: PMID19170179, Figures 5–7.

2. Human relevance is stronger than fibroblast transfer alone. A human PMP22 3′-UTR deletion pedigree shows elevated RNA, and reconstructed deletion reporters resist miR-29a repression. However, the 650-bp deletion spans other predicted miRNA sites; two proband biopsies represent one donor; the reporters are not primary human Schwann cells. This is recovered literature, not a new discovery. Source: PMID37337674 / PMC10545524, Figure 4.

3. IRE1: PMC2728407 Table I explicitly classifies Pmp22 as destabilized under DTT in reconstituted mouse fibroblasts. Published log2 stress responses are −0.88 with restored IRE1 and −0.07 in Ire1-null cells; their calculated difference is −0.81. That is arithmetic on published means, not a new significance test or half-life estimate. Schwann-cell transfer, a PMP22-specific RNase-mutant decay interaction, and a mapped cleavage site remain unverified.

4. A useful counterexample survives: G3BP1 depletion raises PMP22 RNA in MCF-7 cells without a significant reported half-life change. This argues against automatically interpreting an RBP-associated abundance change as altered stability. Nonsignificance is not equivalence. Source: PMC3866477, Table 1.

Upstream controls

Dicer processing, differentiation state and ER stress are supported regulatory influences, but their effects cannot be collapsed into a universal direction. Developmental Dicer loss changes Schwann-cell differentiation and lowers Pmp22, unlike the acute cultured-cell derepression result. Bulk miR-29 abundance also does not establish how much is AGO-loaded or active. Sources: PMC2713384, PMC2906453 and PMC3124220.

IGF2BP3/EDC3 stress-dependent regulation and circRNA competition remain explicitly labelled PMP22 hypotheses—not validated PMP22 decay mechanisms. Sources: PMC13431160 and PMC11784151.

Executed result and stopping point

The completed audit contains nine evidence contexts, seven assay classes and 45 accession labels. One GSE289482 processed library was inspected: 95,582 unique feature rows, with accession-labelled identifiers rather than a clean gene-symbol universe. No PMP22 absence, zero, effect size or half-life was inferred.

GSE289482 has unresolved protocol/metadata discrepancies and lacks the required route-specific perturbation and chase design. GSE118660 was not combined with unrelated footprint experiments. The synthesis/decay/translation decomposition therefore remains unidentified in the audited pairings—not universally impossible.

Proposed decisive test

In state-matched Schwann cells, combine nascent RNA with multi-timepoint labelled-RNA loss, crossing miR-29-site disruption/anti-miR with ERN1 loss and WT/RNase-inactive rescue. A decay explanation predicts faster PMP22 disappearance rescued by the appropriate intervention. Adequately precise equivalence of survival despite RNA loss, together with reduced nascent output, would reject increased decay as sufficient for that condition. This experiment was proposed, not performed.
