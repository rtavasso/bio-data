Total PMP22 is not a dependable proxy for productive delivery. The audited studies separate total abundance, surface fraction and intracellular disposition; they do not establish functional myelin rescue.

Main findings

1. RER1 is the clearest discriminating case. The HEK293 study reports increased surface-trafficking efficiency alongside reduced total PMP22, except possibly N41Q. Those opposing changes do not identify absolute surface abundance. In HeLa cells, Rer1 knockdown redirects L16P predominantly toward Lamp1-positive endosomes/lysosomes; G150D does not respond similarly. ER exit is therefore not sufficient evidence of useful incorporation. Sources: PMC8191293, Results p0175/Figure 6; PMC4227013, Figure 3 and supplements S4–S6.

2. UGGT1 surveillance is not uniformly inhibitory. Its loss reduces trafficking efficiency and total levels for glycosylated PMP22, with N41Q spared. PMC8191293’s abstract contradicts its detailed Results p0170 and Discussion p0210 on this direction; the audit follows the detailed results and preserves the discrepancy.

3. Increased abundance can reflect retained accumulation. MG132 and Hrd1 knockdown accumulate ER-containing PMP22 pools without demonstrating functional delivery. Calnexin effects depend on variant and experimental context. Strong LMAN1 co-IP enrichment produces little trafficking phenotype after knockout—an explicit warning against treating interaction strength as functional necessity. Sources: PMC4227013, Figures 1–2; PMC8191293, Figures 5–6.

Discriminating test

The saved design tests acute RER1 depletion with independent reagents and resistant addback. Measure total and surface PMP22 per viable cell, surface/total fraction, calibrated pulse-labelled synthesis, subsequent surface/myelin delivery yield, endolysosomal routing, aggregation and myelin function.

    Lower total, higher absolute surface and synthesis-normalized myelin yield,
    with improved function → supports productive redistribution.

    Higher surface fraction without higher absolute surface or functional yield,
    especially with increased lysosomal routing → supports loss of retained
    protein or degradative escape, not rescue.

Human wild-type duplication/isogenic correction must be tested separately from L16P coding-variant disease. This experiment is proposed, not performed.
