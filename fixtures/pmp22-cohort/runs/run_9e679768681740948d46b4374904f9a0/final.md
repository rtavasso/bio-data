Main findings

1. ACSL5: increased secreted reporter activity, not merely a smaller denominator.
   Supernatant HRP activity was 1.448/1.321-fold across two independent repeats; intracellular activity was 1.000/0.974-fold. The descriptive 1.25-fold rule survives the tested non-targeting-control/background combinations, but is threshold-sensitive and fails against untreated controls. Without matched viability and protein-mass measurements, this remains a cargo-handling hypothesis—not established secretion flux or PMP22 rescue.

2. The KCNQ1 corrector does not demonstrate PMP22 improvement.
   VU0494372 gave PMP22 surface geometric fold 0.932, with a 95% unpaired model interval of 0.731–1.189, n=3 per arm. Total protein and trafficking efficiency changed differently. This is not equivalence evidence: compound selection already used PMP22 responses, pairing is unresolved, and the workbook’s vehicle label conflicts with the caption.

3. Separate endpoints expose contradictory “secretion hits.”
   TMEM220-targeting siRNA reduced secreted HRP activity to 0.328/0.160-fold while intracellular activity increased to 1.491/1.128-fold—a retention-like pattern. GPR161 reduced secreted activity in both repeats, but its secretion/intracellular ratio reversed direction, from 0.338 to 1.625. Averaging would conceal that difference.

4. Reduced glycan surveillance is not uniformly beneficial.
   Native IGF1R pulse-chase data showed mean mature fractions at two hours of 57.92% for WT, 46.62% for UGGT1 knockout and 21.21% for ALG6 knockout. These are fractions of surviving labeled cargo, not absolute delivery yields or PMP22 measurements.

Validation and novelty

The contribution is endpoint- and repeat-specific reanalysis of published measurements, not a verified new mechanism. Known ACSL5 hormone and lipid-secretion findings provide alternative explanations, not independent validation of general protein transport. The frozen independent cargo prediction remains unevaluated because the candidate validation measurements were inaccessible.

The next discriminating question is whether ACSL5 depletion increases viable-cell-normalized secreted protein mass or pulse-labeled delivery, rather than only HRP activity. PMP22 and functional myelin transfer would require separate tests.
