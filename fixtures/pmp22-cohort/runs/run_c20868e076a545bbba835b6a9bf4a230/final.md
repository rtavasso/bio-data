Both gene-regulatory and protein-environment effects have support, but the audited measurements do not identify which predominates. PMP22 itself can drive lipid abnormalities; a closed transcriptional feedback loop remains unproven.

Main findings

1. LXR/NAC protein recovery is not normal anatomical restoration.
   PMC5802790 reports no detected P21 deficits, unlike adult nerves. NAC began at P21, before the adult phenotype. Although total PMP22/MPZ recovered, small axons became hypermyelinated, axon-caliber distributions changed markedly, and conduction remained complex. The comparison lacks matched promoter-specific nascent RNA, RNA decay and protein-turnover measurements. It establishes neither promoter rescue nor NRF2 necessity.

2. Endocrine responses cannot be reduced to a simple progesterone-receptor mechanism.
   PMID9751213 reports progesterone-responsive P1—but not P2—reporters. RU486 failed to block the response and activated reporters itself. Separate studies report steady-state RNA responses in male transgenic rats and sex/metabolite-specific Schwann-cell responses. These are distinct endpoints and contexts, not endogenous promoter-initiation proof. Several studies remain abstract-limited because full-text retrieval failed.

3. Reverse PMP22-to-lipid causation is supported.
   PMID31061090 reports impaired ABCA1 localization and cholesterol efflux after PMP22 loss despite increased ABCA1/apoE abundance. Reciprocal ABCA1 loss alters PMP22 abundance/processing. This supports bidirectional protein–lipid coupling, not demonstrated transcriptional feedback. I did not repeat the inherited EndoH summary-product calculation as new evidence.

4. The FDFT1 quantitative blocker remains.
   No eligible sample-level genotype matrix was recovered. PMC13042600’s mouse Dhh-Cre primary-culture comparison must not be replaced by its cited GSE216665 rat injury reference or adult Plp-CreERT2 arm. Selected displays do not establish PMP22 absence or zero.
