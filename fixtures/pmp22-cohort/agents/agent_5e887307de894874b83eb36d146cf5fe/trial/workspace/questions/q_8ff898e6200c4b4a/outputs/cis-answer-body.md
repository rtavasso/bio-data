Main findings

1. Endogenous distal enhancer deletion is stronger evidence than a reporter.
   The rat S16-derived study includes allele-specific P1/P2 RT-qPCR, deletion-negative clones, an intact reporter-tagged allele, and Mpz/Mag/Tvp23b controls. These favor a cis effect over a purely shared differentiation response. P1 is more sensitive, but P2 also decreases. The endpoint is steady-state RNA, not nascent initiation; the cell line is polyploid, and enhancer dosage is deliberately changed. Source: PMC6077802 and the author-deposited Pantera thesis, Chapter 2, Figures 2-3/2-4.

2. Human endogenous promoter editing exists, but assay contexts must remain separate.
   PMC7145652 measures total PMP22 after TATA-box editing in primary human Schwann cells. Its promoter-specific P1/P2 measurements come from a different experiment: C22 mouse cultures carrying human transgenes. Combining these does not demonstrate endogenous-human P1-selective initiation. A September 27, 2026 human iPSC study, PMID42801440, supplies another relevant abstract-only lead—not a verified nascent endpoint.

3. Several stronger “direct-promoter” interpretations fail the distinction.
   Intronic +11kb and distal reporter/occupancy experiments do not establish endogenous human necessity. SOX10 depletion also lowers EGR2, leaving indirect regulation possible.
